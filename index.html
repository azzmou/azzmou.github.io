Background

My work sits where software meets a physical machine. I write ROS 2 and C++ for mobile robots and manipulators, program Siemens PLCs for industrial lines, and I am comfortable with an oscilloscope and a soldering iron when the problem turns out to be in the hardware.

That last part is not an afterthought. Before engineering I spent seven years repairing phones at board level, self-employed and alongside full-time study. It taught me to find a fault by measurement rather than by guessing, which is the same habit a robot needs when the simulation works and the real thing does not.

Selected projects

2025 team of 7 lead: robot & integration

PoC-1 Skyrover

Drone–rover coordination over 5G · 5G Academy, Università di Napoli Federico II

A drone and a ground rover working as one system. The drone's pose becomes navigation goals for the rover through a ROS 2 bridge that smooths the signal and applies hysteresis, so the rover is not chasing every twitch of the estimate.

I wrote a custom A* planner and costmap layer for Nav2, and a PPO reinforcement-learning module for rover navigation. We validated in Gazebo and Unreal Engine, then put it on a physical rover and drone, which is where the interesting failures showed up.

2026 C++98

robokin

Manipulator kinematics library · personal project

Forward kinematics, geometric Jacobian, singularity analysis and trajectory generation for serial manipulators, with no external dependencies: the matrix algebra and the Jacobi eigensolver are written out rather than imported, so every step can be checked.

The Jacobian is implemented twice, analytically and by central differences, and the test suite checks one against the other. The demo walks a PUMA 560 through both its boundary and wrist singularities.

2025 BSc thesis

Smart Mobile Base AI

Autonomous mobile base with embedded vision · Università di Napoli Federico II

A mobile robot that tracks an object in real time on low-cost edge hardware. I implemented tracking twice — a learned EfficientDet Lite model and classical HSV segmentation — so the two could be compared on the same frames under the same timing budget.

Running inference on a Raspberry Pi meant the loop time, 100 to 400 ms depending on mode, drove most of the design decisions.
